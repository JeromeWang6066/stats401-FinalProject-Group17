What counts as an overtake?

Broadcasts treat the overtake count as a score for how good a race was. The OpenF1 overtakes feed looks like that score. A row in it can be a pass on track, a pit stop, a retirement, a safety-car shuffle, or a change written down after the flag.

19,303 tagged swaps · 88 sessions (70 races and 18 sprints) · 24 grands prix · OpenF1

Questions

The page is for STATS401 reviewers, and for an F1 viewer who has been told that a season had a lot of overtakes. The feed is large enough that a sorted table does not show who passed whom, on which lap, at which corner, and what share of a named race is left once the bookkeeping is removed.

Five questions organize the charts. How much of the 2023 to 2025 feed is a true on-track pass, and how much is a pit, a retirement, a neutralized period, a formation lap, or a post-race change? After that split, do the remaining passes sit in a few pairs? Do swaps pile up on particular laps, especially the start and the restarts? When they do, is that cluster actually turn 1? And does a driver or team that appears often in the pass records also gain places in the race or in the championship?

A true pass here means both cars swapped on track during a live race. Lap 1, a same-second burst, and an 8-second bounce-back still count. Pits, retirements, formation laps, changes after the chequered flag, and unlapping under a safety car do not.

Data

The source is OpenF1, the unofficial timing API, free from 2023 (api.openf1.org/v1). src/fetch_openf1.py lists every session_type=Race weekend in 2023, 2024, and 2025, then pulls overtakes, drivers, pit, race_control, laps, and position into data/raw/. The track map also uses location for the overtaking car, cut to that session. Season rank uses Jolpica after-round standings. Corner numbers come from each meeting's circuit_info_url.

src/process_overtakes.py joins driver names, abbreviations, teams, and team colours, then places each swap on a lap from lap-start times. A second with at least three swaps is marked burst. If either driver pitted within 90 seconds, the row is near_pit. Race-control messages mark virtual safety car, safety car, and red-flag intervals. A message that the race will resume closes a red flag. "Virtual safety car" does not open or close a real safety car. The chequered flag marks post-race rows. Those checks can overlap on one row. The chart tag does not: each swap gets one of on_track, pit, retired, under_sc, unlap_under_sc, formation, or post_race. The tagged table is data/processed/overtakes_tagged.csv. Field notes are in processed-overtake-data.html.

Five views, one definition

The order is who, then when, then whether those people gained places, then where on the circuit, then what share of the count survives. The Team and Driver buttons under the navigation redraw all five from one choice. Pick a team and the driver list keeps only people who raced under that team name in 2023, 2024, or 2025. Pick a driver and the charts keep swaps that include that driver. All clears the choice. While a choice is active, the bump chart's own team and driver menus wait.

1. Who passes whom

Each node is a driver. Colour is the team colour and size is net overtakes in the current filter. An edge is a pair. Thickness and red are scaled to the smallest and largest counts on screen, so a thick edge means "often, among what is drawn now." Season, grand prix (one or many), swap type, and the exchange slider change which pairs remain. Hover a node for the driver, or an edge for both directions. This is the check on whether overtakes stay among a few pairings after the tag changes.

5. What share is a pass

One race, one sprint, or a whole season. Each slice is one exclusive tag, so the shares add to 100%. The hole is the swap count and the true-pass share. Across all 19,303 rows the two large slices are pit (46.2%) and on-track (43.3%). A season that "had many overtakes" can still be a season that had many pit stops. Click a slice or a table row to hold that tag on the network and the map. Click it again, or All tags, to restore every tag. Click the true-pass slice to replace the ring with lap 1, same-second burst, and 8-second bounce counts. Those three overlap, so they are not a second pie. Click the empty middle to bring the ring back. Changing the session here moves the other four views onto that race. Reading the set

The data on each row are two drivers, a time, a lap when one can be assigned, a track position when location exists, a team colour, and one exclusive tag. The tasks are comparison across tags, spotting a lap cluster, seeing which pairs remain, checking a corner, and reading one race's mix. The idioms are a network (links, with width and colour for count and node size for net overtakes), squares along a lap axis, step lines for rank, a circuit with point marks, and a donut for parts of a whole. Two of those are the heavier representations: the network, and the circuit drawn from car location.

Hover text names the mark under the pointer. The controls change the definition or the subject. You can filter by season, grand prix, and tag; set a minimum number of exchanges; select one team or one driver for every chart; zoom and pan the map; and switch the bump and the donut between a season and one session. Choosing a session in the donut moves the network, the lap chart, the bump, and the map onto that race.

Limits

The exclusive tag is the right object for the donut, because the slices have to add to 100%. It is the wrong object if you want every condition at once. A swap near a pit can also sit in a burst. Those flags stay on the processed row. They do not both appear as slices.

Team colour and team name follow the year of the chart you are looking at. AlphaTauri and RB are different names. Alfa Romeo and Kick Sauber are different names. The driver drawer, once a team is selected, lists anyone who used that name in any of the three seasons. A driver picked from that list is then followed in every year, including years when they drove for someone else.

With a team or driver selected, the donut ignores swaps that have no lap number, and it does not open the lap-1 / burst / bounce split. On the map, a point far from the racing line is drawn without a corner. Edge width in the network is relative to the pairs on screen, so a thick line in a one-race filter is not the same count as a thick line in a full season.

We have not run a classmate test on this page. The task it can support is small: find one true pass, find one cluster that shrinks when pits or a neutralized band are removed, and write down which filter you used. Record the answer, the time, the wrong click, and which control actually helped.

What to take from it

In this feed, a pit swap is more common than a true pass: 8,909 rows against 8,351, out of 19,303. The overtake number quoted for a season is not a count of cars going by each other on track. The lap chart is where that gap shows up inside one race. The network is where you see whether the passes that remain belong to a few pairs. The map is where an early-lap pile either is turn 1 or is not. The bump is the check on whether passing often is the same thing as gaining places.
